KI KOT KHUBOR KHASI BAD KI KOT KHHSI

by u Kitbor W. Nongrum.
A Fore ward by Rev J.L. Roy.
The author, u Kitbor W. Nongrum has taten pains to
compile a compendium of Khasi papers and Khasi books
with a view to providing a coherent and developing
account of the tradition of Khasi literature. The book
cannot, however, be treated as a literary survey descri-
bing the general characteristics of the period s literature
in such a way as enabling the reader to trace its growth
and keep his bearings. The book does not provide an
account of contemporary society at its points of contact
with literature.
The first section of the Book deals with the Khasi
papers dating back to 1889 which year the first Khasi paper
entitled U Nongkitkhubor and edited by Rev. William
Williams appeared in print. It was written from a Christian
angle but it contained articles on Khasi religion, marriage
feast according to Khasi society and culture. The Khasi
poem "U Sirlapalang in 51 stanzas composed by Rev.
Morkha Joseph Chyne, the Khasi poet, was published
in this paper. Between 1889 and 1980 eighty six Khasi
papers were added. Some of these articles were written in
literary style and had rich literary contents.
The khasi papers were arranged in a chronological
order containing the main objectives. This type of compila-
tion stimulates the spent of enquiry and research. Such

questions may be raised - What was the reaction between
writer and public? What was the reading public like in its
tastes and make up? How many survive to this day? Is it
dearth of creative writens, lack of training, paucity of
reading public?
The second section deals with the list of Khasi books.
The hills and vales of Khasi Hills were full of Khasi folk
lores, tales of heroism etc and these were woven round
the fire-place and transmitted by word of mouth from one
generation to another, but these were lost in oblivion for
lack of writing medium. The Khasi alphabets in Roman
form come rather too late. It was Rev. Thomas Jones, the
pathfinder, who invented the khasi alphabets and offered
a suitable medium for expression of Khasi thought The
first Khasi books which appeared in print were written in
archaic style but with passing of time many changes in
spelling and accent were introduce like the original c
changed to 'k
The books are arrange starting with the name of the
author and not in a chronological order. Literature is to
be viewed not as a mere academic product but as one
expression of the many - sided activities of national growth,
historical sketch of contemporary social and political
events as a prelude to every important period in the deve-

lopment of any literature. The various stages of literary
development are absent for lack of a proper chronology.
In any anthology not all the books are included. There is
a tesk of selection to be performed based on works which
are of more value than others keeping in view the function
of criticism which in old days was neither respected nor
competently performed.
Yet on the whole the compilation is to be highly
commended.
