sa InToNATroN sA Utterance final intonation is characterized by a low-falling pitch on the last
syllable. other syllables are on a mid pitch. Question intonation is characterized by "a sharp
rise in pitch on the last syllable and/or a hish pitch on the Ipenultimate syllablel with low
fallingi pitch on the next,it (p.35) Before a pause, in a continuins utterance the intonation is
characterized by "a series of pitches gradually falling from mid to low and a quick rise from
low to mid on the [finall syilable." (p.36) Consult the source (p.33-37) for further details.

sa vouels sA Rabel's analysis of the Khasi vowel system is peculiar. The phonemes she sets up
bear little relationship to the phones and their distribution as described by her. In her
phonemic analysis (p.t3) there are five long and five short vowels. However, it is clear from
the examples and discussion of distribution that vowel length is distinctive only for the low
vowel. otherwise vowels are lons finally and before /r-trill/, and short elsewhere. Rabel also
uses a phonemic distinction in length to indicate a purely qualitative distinction. She
analyzes the difference between final li-longl and Te-long) and the difference between final
[u-long] and lo-longl as, /i.i/ vs. /i/ and /u.u/ vs. /u/ respectively, when the obvious
analysis would be to call them /i/ and /e/, /u/ and /o/ respectively. The vowel system as
presented by the Archive is a reanalysis alons these lines.' tJHcl
