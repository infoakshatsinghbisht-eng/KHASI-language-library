Introduction 

The First reference to sonnets in Khasi prose which survives 
till today was made by the Rev. Father Hopewell Elias (1973) ; it 
was followed by an opinion of Owen Rowie on the sonnets of Dewi 
Sing Khongdup (1967). The work of Elias simply speaks of the 
sonnet as a peotic form and that of Rowie appreciates the accom- 
plished sonnets of our poet Khongdup. So the field was left open to 
Mr. B. L. Swer to investigate the origin of this form of poetic cre- 
ation to describe its features, and to analyse its peotic and theme 
with understanding and confidence. 

Mr. Swer was meditating upon the topic for some weeks in 
August this year for a college seminar. But when I perused, the scheme 
he has chosen to follow,. I found that he has delved deep enough and 
has analysed the materials he came by in a schematic manner. He has 
done well to have it published when zeitgist demands for more works 
of this catogory in our own language. 

Dessident voices respecting the whole work should not deter 
many a tyro in this field of study, for ‘He that observeth the wind 
shall not sow ; and he that regardeth the clouds shall not reap.” A

critic should on the other hand always cast his bread upon the waters 
; for he shall find it after many days. Readers who are used to read- 
ing voluminous works may find this a monicum, but the great ness of 
a work of art does not lie alw ays on volumes of printed pages for it is 
also found in the justification of the title by printed words and the 
quality of such words w'hich this booklet is not Jacking. 

This is the fiist authentic historical account of the sonnet in 
Khasi, the first critical study of Khongdup's sonnet of this length 
and magnitude, and it happens also to be the first work in which Mr. 
Swer demonstrates his critical faculty. It is, therefore, premature to 
post it into any ledger folio of known schools, of criticism because 
the author is yet to prepare his manifesto to that effects ; but Katto 
Katne Shaphang ka Sonnet is a positive contribution of Khasi liter- 
ary criticism which should be welcomed. 

Garikhana, Shillong H. W. STEN

To Sleep 


A flock of sheep that leisurely pass by, 

One after one ; the sound of rain and bees 
Murmuring ; the fail of rivers, winds and seas. 
Smooth fields, white sheets of water and pure sky;

I have thought of all by turns and yet do lie 
Sleepless ! and soon the small birds’ melodies 
Must hear, first uttered from my orchard trees ; 
And the first cuckoo’s melancholy cry. 

Even thus last night, and two nights more, I lay 
And could not win thee, Sleep ! by any stealth ; 

So do not let me wear tonight away. 

Without thee what is all the morning’s wealth ? 
Come, blessed barrier between day and day, 

Dear mother of fresh thought and joyous health.

When to the sessions of sweet silent thought 
I summon up remembrance of things, 

I sigh the lack of many a thing I sought, 

And with old woes new wail my dear time’s waste, 

Then can I drown an eye, unuse’d to flow 

For precious friends, bid in death’s dateless night; 

And weep afresh love’s long since cancell’d woe, 

And moan the expenses of many a vanished sight 
Then can I grieve at grievances foregone. 

And heavily from woe to woe tell o’er 
The sad account of fore-bemoaned moan, 

Which I now pay as if not paid before.

But if the while I think of the dear friend, 

All loses are restored, and sorrows end.
