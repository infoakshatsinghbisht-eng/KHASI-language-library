A Tribute to Dr. Homiwell Lyngdoh 

The 26th February 1977 is the birth centenary of Dr, 
Homiwell Lyngdoh (Nonglait). Among the luminaries of 
the age who contributed largely to the literary world of 
the Khasis were U Soso Tham, U Rev. Father Elias 
Hopewell Sohlia and U Dr. Homiwell Lyngdoh, It was 
these three who spearheaded and projected the icpportance 
of the Modern Khasi Literature during the years from 
1914 to 1940. The Modern Khasi literature owes a great 
deal to these three stalwarts for their contributions. It 
was largely as a result of their organised and indefatigable 
efforts that the Khasi literature is getting a due place of 
importance in the field of literature today. 

To this victory. Dr. LyngdoH however contributed in 
a manner that is distinctive of the man. Unlike some of 
his eminent comtemporaries, Dr* ILyogdoh drew on his 
architectonic genius and historicil acumen to build up the 
Khasi literature on a strong footing for the benefit of the 
future generations. Dr. Lyngdoh is a great name in the 
Khasi literature, history and culture. He was the first

Khasi doctor in Medicines who graduated from' the Cal- 
cutta Medical College in 1903 and was the first Khasi Civil 
Surgeon who served in different parts of Assam for many 
years. He was also the first Khasi Officer to be commis' 
stoned in the Indian Medical Service where he served for 
four years from 1917 to 1921 and held the rank of a cap- 
tain. In the midst of arduous cask as a physician, he ren- 
dered selfless life-long devotion to the cause of Khasi lite- 
rature, culture and history. He played an important role 
In the historical investigation of the origin of the Khasis as 
revealed from bis book entitled *'Ka Niam Khasi.’* 

Although a physician^ Dr. Lyngloh was a born lite- 
rateur. The first book he published was on health and 
hygiene which saw the light of day in 1914. In 1928 he 
published a book entitled , **Ka Shad Nongkrem bad Thang 
Syiem Sohra.” In 193^» Dr. Lyngdoh wrote a small 
booklet entitled **The Short Accounts of Cherrapunji**

which whs presented t6 Lord Welltngdon. the then Gover- 
nor General & Viceroy of India during his v'sit to Cher- 
rapuoji in the same year. He travelled widely over the 
IChasi A laintia Hills, collected valuable information from 
the age of folk-lore to his own times. Afterwards the 
work induced Dr. Lyngdoh to embark on a far more ambi- 
tious project. With the materials be collected he wrote 
the books on “tCa Niam ICbasi** and “Ki Syiem Kbasi & 
oynteng** which were published in 1937 and 1938 respec- 
tively. The publication of these two books was then a 
great event in the history of the Khasi literature. As the 
result of his literary research the Kbasi literature was thus

introduced, for the first time in the thirties, as a subject of 
higher education in the Calcutta University. His success 
to introduce Khasi as a subject for study in the graduate 
courses thus became one of Dr. Lyngdoh’s achievements. 
It was due to bis indefatigable energy that a strong foun- 
dation was laid for future historical research in the field of 
Khasi culture. Dr. Lyngdoh was a leading literary figure 
of the age. His writings were easily communicative« but 
always elegant. He brought the historical perspective very 
ably and diligently. Hie writings reveal a descriptive yet 
deep inquisitive bias in his works. He was indeed one of 
the legendary trio that shaped the beginnings of Modern 
Khasi Literature.
