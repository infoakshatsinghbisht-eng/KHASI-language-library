FOREWORD. 
Ill 1939, not long after I had started edncational work 
ih the Khasi Hills, I was taken by the monks of the Rama- 
krishna Mission at Cherrapunji to visit the New India 
School at Sohbar. There I tound the children singing a type 
of song that I had not heard before, Indian national songs 
with Khasi words and set to Indian tunes. I was so delighted 
with these that I got the children to teach me there and then 
the one beginning "Ko Mei Ri India", so that I could take it 
back to Shillong and teach it to the children in my school. 
Later I discovered that this song was one of many that 
had been composed in Khasi by Jiten Maharaj (now Swami 
Chandikanandal of the Ramakrishna Mission. 
For many years I have wanted a book contu’ning these 
songs, and now, with the coming of Basic Education to the 
Khasi Hills, we have decided that the time has come to 
publish such h book. For the words of these song* is com- 
pletely in harmony with Basic Education, combining as they 
do the best type of patrotism, the loftiest idealism and the 
widest universalism. And being set to Indian tunes they help 
these Hill children, whose education has hitherto been far 
too westernised, to get the beginning of understanding and 
appreciation of Indian music, 
I hope that this little book will spreadjfar and widei 
throughout the Khasi Hills as the song book of the Basitj 
Schools and that these words and tunes will find a place in

the hearts of the Kh*si people along with those of their own 
beautiful but all too few folk songs, and so help to make them; 
realise their oneness with all their fellow -Indians. 
I am most grateful to Swami Chandikananda for alio* 
'wing me publish this small selection of his songs. 
Shillong, 
Margaret Barr
