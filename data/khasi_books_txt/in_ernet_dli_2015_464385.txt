FOREWORD

By Dr. HAMLET BABBH,

Editor, Nagaland Dittriet OaztiUers.

This small anthology of poems composed by Mr. B. C. 
Jyrwa, Jaiaw, Shillong, constitutes another contribution 
yet to Kbasl poetry, for which I have a great pleasure to 
commend it to the intelligentzia. It Is heartening indeed 
that young poets from the community are coming out, and 
although little it may look, their contribution is by no 
means spurious.

‘U Klew* a popular Khasi folk-tale rendered to poetry 
is well written. Jyrwa has amply brought out its themes 
in a dramatic tone unfurling the varied thought and senti- 
ment, cultural emblem, mode of living, kinship ties, and 
religious background in the indigenous fashion which sets 
thrills to an inquisitive reader. It is for this originality of 
things that it has its beauty. It has a parallel in ‘ U Sier- 
lapalang ’ of U Primrose Gatphoh also based on a folk- 
tale, both which contain elegiac themes and which rank as 
classical stories, the pride of our literature.

Modern poets have not yet found it feasible to aban- 
done the lines in modern poetry set down by U Soso Tham, 
the greatest of the Khasi poets three decades ago. It is on 
the background he has laid down, that they persist to probe 
into the origin of evil and sin according to a Khasi concep- 
tion and mode of thinking. And because of that, and the 
variety of subject matter with which it is interconnected, 
their works deserve to be made effective.

The manner in which Jyrwa bandies is laudable in 
respect of the construability of thoughts, metrical assign- 
ment. the mode of expression and the Khasi sentiments 
which govern.

The other eight naive verses and lyrics have worthly 
ethical lessons as Jyrwa speaks with courage against the 
degenerating standards of morality among his country men

and women. A clarion call to improve our lot morally 
and spirituallyi as much as there is still time to do so is 
worthy of note. The letter composition is attempted to be 
and adaptation to the modern conditions in the Khasi 
Society. And behind it, there is a patriotic fervidity which 
deserves the attention of the rising generation in particular.

Dated Sajar Lodge, Kohima. 
April 3, 1969.

Hamubt Bareh.
