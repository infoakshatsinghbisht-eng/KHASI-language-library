I have with great inteiest gone through
this book “ KI NUKSA HA KA JINGIM BA
DEI BAN PHIKIR*' on Khasi ethical lessons
by U larington Kharkongngor.

A spade work was done during the beginning
of this century to explore and present the
Khasi ethical lessons and ideas by Radhou Singh
Berry and Rabon Singh m their publications*
They were the earliest Khasi authors who had
been instrumental for bringing about the
resurgence of letters in the field of Khasi
literature through their works. Since then, other
authors emulating them, have tried to do some-
thing constructive to dig out the Khasi thoughts
and ideas. But Mr. Kharkongngor has done more
Justice to the subject by placing Khasi ethical
thoughts and practices more systematically and
coherently. The author deserves our heartiest
congratulation for his laudable attempts in pre-
senting aspecially this book in a very Khasi style
and fashion.
Following in the foot-steps of his enlightened
ancestors, Mr. Kharkongngor wrote this book to
inspire the present generation with good man-
ners and morals. And from this book, deligent
readers should acquire iustructive lessous to grow
up in line with the ideal code of behaviour.
This book may, therefore, be regarded as one
of the best books yet published on Khasi ethics
and moral lessons. And we shall expect many
more contributions in this subject from other
persons in the days to come. The themes
which the book offers, have a special bearing
particularly in the present day when so many
evils have consumed the life of many young
people. How many persons have wasteil their
life and talents and how many have ruined them-
selves because they have not received adequate
moral instructions ? We should do more to bring
out persons of vision and character from our
society.
It is my fervent wish that this book finds
out its use in schools, libraries and homes.
It will serve best as a supplementary Text-Book
for the use of students op to secondary stage.
Dated Shillong,
January 6, 1966.
