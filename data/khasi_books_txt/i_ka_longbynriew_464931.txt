FOREWORD 


The world is now approaching the close of 
the twentieth century, but it is unfortunate that 
the majority of the male members of the Khasi 
and Pnar community have not yet been able to 
achieve the status of responsible manhood, 
which is the prevailing custom in other parts of 
the world. In our present day society the male 
members of the Khasi and Pnar community 
continue to be subjected to the process of Head- 
shrinking of our tradition and customs, and are 
thereby relegated loan inferior position in rela- 
t ionship to the women-folk of our community. 
The constant contact with other cultural patterns 
makes us constantly aware of our lack of respon- 
sibility, independence and freedom in our social re- 
lationships. The attempt to equate ourselves with 
patrilineal societies only leads to hypocrisy. Social 
and domestic freedom which is such a glorious 
thing is very difficult to achieve, but once it is 
obtained, it will give each one of us added vigour 
to live with purpose and enjoy its lasting fruit 
with a broader outlook on life. It is only then 
that we can lay claim to a life worth-living.

I dare say that in the heart of every Khasi 
and Pnar there is an inborn sense of courage 
and other attractive qualities which unfortunately 
have been hidden and imprisoned under the 
weight of our traditional custom. I therefore, feel 
that the time has come for us to liberate ourselves 
and break away the chains that have kept us impri- 
soned for such a long period of time. 



11 


It would not be wrong in saying that by 
nature, males are more violent and aggressive, 
whereas the females are endowed with the virtues 
of sympathy, love and tolerance. Unfortunately 
contact with other customs and societies have 
not worked in our favour with the result that the 
females of the Khasi and Pnar community have 
now become more aggressive and violent and the 
males have become progressively more tolerant 
and have consequently been reduced to a secon- 
dary position in the society. The world is con- 
stantly moving close together and it is in this 
context I am greatly aggrieved to find that our 
present customs are bringing about a down-fall 
in all spheres of life, particularly in the social, 
economic and political life of our people. I also 
believe that the domestic imbalance in our society 
is largely responsible for our failure to achieve a 
good life right from our own domestic hearths 
upto the highest level of society.

Such failures tormented my heart and made 
me realize that we have a duty to shoulder a 
greater responsibility for the betterment of our 
society. 


(J.D. LYNGDOH )
