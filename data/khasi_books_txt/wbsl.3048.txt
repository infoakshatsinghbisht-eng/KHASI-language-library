3. The Chief Commissioner regrets to observe the, very large
falling off in the number of persons vaccinated during the year, as
compared with the preceding year, the figures being respectively 754
and'1,817. The cause assigned by the Civil Surgeon may possibly
account to some extent for this falling off, but Dr. O'Brien's absence
during the greater part of the cold weather of 1876-77 resulted doubt-
less in a lessened activity on the part of the subordinate agency.
Colonel Keatinge is not yet prepared to introduce compulsory vaccination
into these hills.

stationary.

6. Vaccination has not made favorable progress, the total number
of children vaccinated during the year 1876 is 1,809 against 1,628 of
the preceding year, 1875, showing a decrease of 3i9. Dr. O'Brien, the
Civil Surgeon, attributes this to the fact that a great number of children
had had the small-pox ; he further states that there are many obstacles
which have to be encountered in overcoming the prejudices and apathy
of the people with regard to vaccination, and he advocates compulsory
vaccination ; but considering how, even in civilized England, obstruction
is offered to vaccination by the lower classes, the objection on the part
of the semi-barbarous people of these hills is not to be wondered at.
7. The material condition of the people may be said to be slowly

trade with' the hills, and a larger local demand, the progress will no doubt
be annually more and more evident.
