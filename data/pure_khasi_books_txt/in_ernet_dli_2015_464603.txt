INTRODUCTION

The Year 1979 will go down in the bistory of our nee 
as a year of positive efforts on the parts of literary leaders to 
produce boohs in our own language which the society deser* 
ved long ago. The history of Khasi language was brought 
out in May this year; this morning I prefaced another booh 
of literary criticism ; and I feel somewhat proud that this 
history of Khasi literature also is now made avaliable to our 
reading public. I am happy to be instrumeata] in transfor- 
ming it h'om possible codex into booh.

Ka History ka Tkoh ka Tar is in more ways than one a 
systematic compilation of the material Dr. R. S. Lyngdob 
had used in class-rooms, magazine articles and Radio talks. 
It contains five chapters covering the period of Khasi lite- 
rature from William Carey's mission to John Roberts. The 
author had to rely substantially on the work of G. Anpeli 
Jones and others which he duly acknowledges. The first 
chapter is very original. It gives readers an information 
about the power and beauty of Khasi language, the simila- 
rity of the language with other languages, and hints at dialec- 
tal variation of Khasi. From the second chapter onto the 
fifth, it proceeds with the written history of our literature 
with occasional promises for more volumes to come out. •

Though biographical critics may have their own say on 
the work; a great deal of scholarship exhibited by the author 
will not fail to interest critics of otter schools. Whether or 
not the author takes the mentality of ' the Khasi reading 
public into consideration while writing the book, it has 
special appeal to foreigners also, especially to the Welsh in 
Wales. It will relieve our students from the burden of tran- 
slating Dr. H. Bareb’s A Short History of Khasi Literature 
mentally and practically while preparing for examinations. 
Until current or future research proves otherwise, the fact 
and figutes'given in this book are sure to serve as a stifttal

point for those who engage themselves In meaningful study 
of Khasi literary history. The book is handy, interpretative 
and simple in style, that adding more words into the intro- 
duction may, I am afraid, take away some amount of 
curiousity and thrill of the readers. I better stop here; but 
before long I must say that Svlih thlp Work. Dr. Lyogdoh 
has started filling the gap up which should and could have 
been done fio a few decades ago.
