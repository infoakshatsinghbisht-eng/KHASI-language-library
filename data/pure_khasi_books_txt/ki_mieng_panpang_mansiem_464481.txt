FOREWORD. 


I have great pleasure to introduce these first Khasi 
Sonnets by Jerome Diengdoh. This makes an addition

to Khasi Poetry in this respect. 

Sonnets, though brief and concise, are sweet and have 
their role in instilling a new passion upon the subject which 
they center round. Khasi sonnets have been very few, 
but Jerome Diengdoh has made up the gap by having them 
consistently published in this beautiful anthology. This 
is inspiring as Jerome is still young but in these lines, have 
exploded the thoughts which may have almost reached 
maturity. He has indeed created new models which should 
rank as good creations in Khasi poetry. 

The verses, naive as they are as also forceful and clear, 
carry with them the wide range of imagination, the depth 
of thought and the enlightening power. There is nothing 
obscure although classists also prefer the cryptic elements 
to conjure up with the absolute. 

I have enjoyed reading his love poems, full of charm 
and colour and the imagery which always lead us to the 
yearning sou). It is not only for the outward form, taste 
and temperament but also more for the eternal power of 
love that one should strive for. This should be indeed the , 
moving spirit of poetry. Jerome has also struck deep ' 
the chords in search of a truer meaning of life although 
they flow with the elegiac notes. What is here that we should 
strive on - is it the dull fashion and bargaining for power 
and riches ? true struggles have with them victories and 
defeats. Plain living and high thinking have to be so devised . 
to play their role in the present day-life. 

The themes are so melancholic but they also invoke 
us to find the meaning of life and death. In their search*

11 


many questions are come across on death as a decisive force 
that disposes of the human destiny. My contention of 
course is that peace and happiness are the greatest of the 
blessings of the Heaven and remembering on that beyond 
the cloud, time may come when we see a rainbow. 

Elegiac they may be, yet I have found more than once 
that jests and satires are not entirely absent and they ex- 
plode here and there. It is only the serious reader who 
could grasp their meaning. 

I commend this young poet to our reading public and 
that his sonnets should be intelligently read and their tho- 
ughts be well stored. 

I also wish that more of such creations come out from 
his pen in the days to come. 

North Eastern Hill University, Shillong 
Centre for Continuing Education. 

Dr. Hamlet Bareh,
