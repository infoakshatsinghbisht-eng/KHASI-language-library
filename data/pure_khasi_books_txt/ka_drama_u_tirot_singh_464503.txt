PRANK M. PUGH. AN APPRECIATION Mr. Victor G, Bareh is one of the few Khasi writers who took pains — to bring back to life, certain personalities of undated Khasi history. One of such writings is the drama *'U Tirot Singh*’ which has since been translated into English. Others of his writings sre based on legends and traditions, yet through diligence and painstaking efforts he has produced for the benefit of the reading public in these hills. I shall at present confine my appreciation of "U Tirot Singh" only. «'U Tirot Singh" gives a vivid picture of

the spirit and determination of Tirot Singh, the Syiem of Nongkhiaw, when the British came to these hills. The drama depicts the events leading ftom a friendly relationship of the Khasis with the Ca«-t India Company, to the anti British struggle put up by U Tirot Singh Syiem, and his contemporary, U Bor Manick. While U Tirot Singh was concentrating his fight on the Borduar side, U Bor Manick put up a fierce fight in the Beitoila (Spelt Bortula in the Drama) area.
