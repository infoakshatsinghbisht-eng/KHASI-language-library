Once more D. E. Stevenson has chosen the Scottish 
Borders as a setting for a novel and has given ns 
memorable pictures of moors and forests and of a 
cloud-capped hill which plays an important part in 
the stor>\ Torfoot is a cosy little town where everybody 
knows everybody else and people from the surrounding 
country meet and chat and do their shopping. The hero 
of the story — Will Has tie — comes home from Military 
Service with the intention of settling down on his family 
estate and learning to be a farmer, but before he has 
been at home for long he scents a mystery which sends 
him to France on “ a wild goose chase.** 

The novel is beautifully imagined with a blending of 
romance and spiritual achievement which satisfies while 
it entertains. It has been said that D. E. Stevenson 
“ writes with poetry in her pen ** — and she certainly 
docs — but perhaps her most distinguished achievement 
is the creation and delineation of characters. Here we 
find people so real and lovable that they become our 
friends.
