The district was resettled in 1911-12. The method Resettie- 
of assessment was the now well-known soil-unit system mont ' 
introduced by Sir Bampfylde Puller in 1902 in Kamrup 
and Sibsagar. The application of the old and new rates 
to the area settled at attestation resulted in an increase of 
11’43 per cent, in the rcvonue of the district. In North 
Lakhimpur the enhancement, mainly due to surrey, was 
under 3 per cent, while in the Sadr subdivision after the 
exclusion of the two mauzas which are economically part 
of North Lakhimpur there was an enhancement of 14’8 
per cent. That the now assessment was moderate is 
shown by the fact that it represents only 14 per cent, of 
the value of tho gross produce of tho area cropped. 

Sudden increase in revenue was prevented by the applica¬ 
tion of tho rules for progressive enhancement. 

Nitf-khiraj estates were assessed on cultivation, an.d 
their assessment shows an increase of 15‘2 per cent. 

Lakhiroj lands were classed and rovenue calculated on 
the cultivated area for the purpose of calculating local 
rates.

April 1911 in North Lakhimpur subdivision and in the 
Dibrugarh assessment group, and from April 1912 in the 
rest of the Dibrugarh subdivision.

About the middlo of March 1911, Mr. Noel William- 
son, Assistant Political Officer at Sadiya, accompanied ]ifl r rason a nd 
by Dr. J. D. Gregorson and a considerable number of his party, 
servants and coolies started on a tour in the Abor 
country. The party left Passighat on the 18th March 
and arrived at ltotung on the 20th. A few days later 
it was split up into two, Mr. Williamson going on a 
little ahead, while Dr. Gregorson stayed behind with 
some sick coolies. On the 30th March Dr. Gregorson’s 
party was treacherously attacked at Pangi by the Abors, 
and Dr. Gregorson and practically all those with him 
were murdered. The same fate overtook Mr. William¬ 
son and his men on the following day at Komsing. Of 
all those present only five coolies escaped with their 
lives. 

The season was now too far advanced to despatch Aborexpe- 
immediately a punitive expedition into this little of 

known and difficult country. Enquiries were however 
at once instituted to discover what villages were respon¬ 
sible, and in the following October a strong force under 
General Bower started to avenge the massacre, and

A further and not the least important result of the 
Abor Expedition was the formation of tho North-East 
Erontier District. Eormerly portion of this district 
was known as the Dibrugarh Erontier Tract, and was 
administered by an Assistant Political Offioer with 
headquarters at Sadiya, subordinate to the Deputy 
Commissioner, Lakhimpur. After tho expedition of 
1911-12, this system of administration was changed. 
The Dibrugarh Frontier Tract ceased to exist, and the 
district of Sadiya now considerably enlarged became 
a separate entity controlled by a Political Officer, work¬ 
ing directly under the Chief Commissioner, with three 
Assistant Political Officers; of these one is posted at 
Passighat, and is broadly speaking in charge of the 
Abor Hills. A detailed description of the boundary 
of the newly formed district will be found in the 
Government of India Gazette Notification No. 977E.-B., 
dated the 25th September 1914. Further modifications 
of the district boundary will he found described in 
Notifications Nos. 978 and 979E-B. of the same date.
